# Lens: Grounding & Provenance (2025-2026)

## Findings

1. **Citation "correctness" != "faithfulness."** Wallat et al. formalize faithful citation as three conditions: the cited doc is in the retrieved context, it supports the statement, and the statement is *causally* impacted by the doc (removing it would change the output). Empirically, up to 57% of citations in attributed RAG answers fail the faithfulness test even though they look correct on the surface — models often generate from parametric memory then attach a citation that superficially fits ("post-rationalization"). https://aclanthology.org/2025.clicit-1.23.pdf (also framed as "Correctness is not Faithfulness in Retrieval Augmented Generation")

2. **No detector beats 50/50 precision+recall simultaneously on RAGTruth.** RAGTruth (~18K word-level annotated RAG responses across LLMs) shows that even GPT-4-based prompted detectors cannot get both precision and recall above 50% for summarization/QA hallucination detection. Fine-tuning a small LLM on RAGTruth annotations beats prompt-based GPT-4 judging and, deployed in-pipeline, measurably reduces hallucinations. https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.585/

3. **Self-consistency (multi-sample agreement) is a weak hallucination signal.** Black-box detection research documents "tiers of hallucination that elude detection through self-consistency checks" — models can be confidently, consistently wrong across resampled generations when they hold a strong but false prior. https://arxiv.org/html/2311.01740v2

4. **Semantic entropy is the leading calibration technique for open-ended generation.** Farquhar et al.'s method clusters sampled answers into semantic equivalence classes and computes entropy over that distribution rather than raw token probability, giving a much better correctness-correlated confidence signal — but it still fails when a model is systematically, confidently wrong (low semantic entropy on a wrong answer). https://arxiv.org/html/2406.13663v1 (Nature-published; widely cited 2025-2026 as the calibration baseline)

5. **Dedicated hallucination-detection models beat general LLM judges.** Vectara's HHEM v2 (Hughes Hallucination Evaluation Model) gives a 0-1 "Factual Consistency Score" and hits ~73% accuracy on AggreFact's SOTA subset, beating a ChatGPT-based LLM judge by ~10 points, while being far cheaper/lower-latency to run at scale than asking a frontier LLM to self-judge. https://www.vectara.com/blog/hhem-v2-a-new-and-improved-factual-consistency-scoring-model

6. **"Mirage" verifies citations via model internals, not self-report.** Instead of asking the LLM to cite itself (which produces non-existent or mismatched sources), Mirage detects context-sensitive answer tokens and attributes them to the retrieved documents that causally influenced those tokens via saliency analysis, then converts that into citations — high agreement with human attribution judgments on multilingual extractive QA. This is one of the few approaches that mechanically verifies use, not just topical overlap. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16198 (ALCE/Mirage line of work)

7. **VeriCite and SemanticCite are explicit post-hoc citation verification layers**, distinct from generation. VeriCite validates that cited evidence genuinely supports a claim after the RAG answer is produced. SemanticCite does full-text retrieval within cited papers and classifies each claim-source pair into Supported / Partially Supported / Unsupported / Uncertain — a 4-way scheme (not binary), released with a 1,000+ citation benchmark across 8 disciplines, and shows fine-tuned small models match large commercial systems on this task. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3731120.3744592 ; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3767695.3769505

8. **Anthropic's Claude Citations API only guarantees pointer correctness, not semantic faithfulness.** Citations are structurally tied to real document spans (page ranges for PDFs, char ranges for text) so they can't reference non-existent sources, and cited text doesn't count toward output tokens — but Anthropic's own docs don't claim an independent entailment/support check runs on top. It solves the "hallucinated citation" problem, not the "citation doesn't actually support the claim" problem. https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations

9. **Most mainstream consumer products (Perplexity, Gemini AI Overviews/Search grounding, Bing Copilot, ChatGPT search, You.com) display citations as retrieval pointers, not mechanically-verified support.** Public documentation for these products emphasizes retrieval + inline linking ("verify the source," "check the evidence") but none publicly describe a claim-level entailment/verification pass equivalent to HHEM, VeriCite, or SemanticCite. The mechanical-verification layer is currently concentrated in research prototypes and specialized enterprise platforms (Vectara), not consumer chat UX. https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/products/search ; https://cloud.google.com/discover/what-are-ai-hallucinations ; https://stanford.service-now.com/services?id=kb_article&number=KB00020824

10. **Google's own enterprise guidance for Gemini Search grounding explicitly instructs "trust but verify."** Stanford's internal KB tells staff to "read inline citations in the Gemini response and open each source to verify it," and if a citation resolves to a secondary source rather than the primary announcement, to re-prompt Gemini to find the primary source — i.e., the product vendor's own operational guidance assumes citations are a starting point for human verification, not a guarantee. https://stanford.service-now.com/services?id=kb_article&number=KB00020824

11. **NotebookLM's trust pitch is scope-limiting, not verification.** NotebookLM markets itself as answering "only from what you upload, with receipts" — the trust mechanism is corpus restriction (can't hallucinate outside your docs) plus citations, not an independent faithfulness check on top of generation.

12. **Hallucination taxonomy matters for detection design.** Distinguish intrinsic (internally incoherent) vs extrinsic (inconsistent with source/reality) hallucinations, and within extrinsic: unsupported claims, contradictory claims, and fabricated references (citations to non-existent docs, wrong authorship, misquotes). RAGTruth and SemanticCite both operate on this granularity rather than a single binary "hallucinated/not." https://cloud.google.com/discover/what-are-ai-hallucinations

13. **Benchmark ceiling is well below "safe to fully automate."** Best detector accuracy on AggreFact's SOTA subset is ~70.2%, SummaC's best is 75.8% — meaning roughly 1 in 4 to 1 in 3 classifications are wrong even with the best current dedicated detectors, reinforcing that citation/gating systems today are decision-support, not autonomous fact-checkers.

14. **UX design literature frames uncertainty-handling as a first-class AI product problem**, not just a backend accuracy problem — designing for graceful degradation, abstention, and "handling user frustration with AI" when confidence is low, rather than presenting all answers with uniform authority. https://medium.muz.li/a-designers-guide-to-ui-ux-patterns-for-ai-products-series-6-handling-user-frustration-with-ai-c5912e2cd385
